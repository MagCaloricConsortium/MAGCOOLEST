Research

MAGCOOLEST runs along two complementary routes: understanding how engineered gradients change the caloric response of a material, and proving that response in working devices.

General objectives

Understanding graded alloys

Advance the understanding of synthesis routes and of the thermal, mechanical and magnetic behaviour of heterostructures and of compositionally and morphologically graded iron-, nickel- and manganese-based alloys. Controlled hydrogenation, diffusion and evaporation are used to tune where each phase transition happens inside a single piece of material.

Surviving the powder route

Assess what happens to alloys lean in critical raw materials once they are milled into powder and printed. Durability and mechanical integrity matter as much as caloric performance if the material is to end up inside a heat exchanger.

Proving it in devices

Validate energy harvesting and solid-state refrigeration by designing, integrating and testing demonstrators built from transition-temperature-graded materials, assessed under realistic operating conditions.

Work packages

WP1 · Production of parent magnetocaloric materials

Computational pre-screening by density functional theory, molecular dynamics and machine learning, followed by synthesis of polycrystalline alloys through arc and induction melting, and of single crystals, thin films and melt-spun ribbons.

WP2 · Grading and mechanical reinforcement

Discrete composites consolidated by spark plasma sintering, continuous composition gradients produced in an optical floating-zone furnace or by hydrogenation, morphological gradients through powder metallurgy and additive manufacturing, and ductile metallic coatings where mechanical strength falls short.

WP3 · Additive manufacturing

Controlled-atmosphere milling into printable powders, formulation of inks and pastes with the right viscosity and particle size, and printing by screen printing, direct ink writing and laser powder bed fusion while preserving caloric performance.

WP4 · Heat exchangers and generator demonstrators

Computational fluid dynamics design of an active magnetic regenerator, plus thermal and mechanical energy harvesting demonstrators, including graded-porosity foams that convert vibration into electrical output.

WP5 · Multifunctional characterisation

Structural, thermal, magnetic and magnetoelastic characterisation in the three laboratories, extended with synchrotron radiation, neutron scattering and pulsed high magnetic fields at international facilities, and supported by Monte Carlo and micromagnetic modelling.

WP6 · Coordination and outreach

Coordination between the three subprojects, training of early-career researchers, energy-efficiency and risk review, dissemination, and management of data and intellectual property.

Materials under study

  • La(Fe,Mn,Si)13Hx and MnFePSi alloys and composites, for room-temperature regenerators
  • Fe–Rh and Heusler-type magnetic shape memory alloys, for magneto- and elastocaloric effects
  • R2Fe17 intermetallics and Fe–Zr–B amorphous alloys, for magnetovolume-driven energy harvesting
